## Deep-Dive Group A · Q3 of Cecil's closing questions

**The question (Cecil's words, posed at 59:53 of "The Ancients: Vyasa and The Mahabharata," 19 Jan 2018): "What are people worth?"**

*Method: a FOR case and an AGAINST case were built independently, each bound by the Cecil Rule — nothing implied, nothing inferred, everything in evidence, all claims tied to the movements and timestamps of the house record (CECIL-MAHABHARATA-RECORD.md). The CROSS at the end compares them.*

---

## FOR — the epic's answer, at full strength

**1. The thesis is stated as a definition, not a sentiment — People as true wealth chapter, 24:48.**
Cecil opens the movement by redefining the measure of a life: "the greatness of your life" (24:48) — its joy, pleasure, power, and riches — "are the people in your life." On that definition, people are not one good among others, to be balanced against money or status. They are the substance in which greatness, if it exists, consists. To ask what people are worth is therefore not to ask their price. It is to ask what remains when they are removed.

**2. The poem proves the definition by subtraction — the forest exile, Vana Parva movement after 24:48.**
The five Pandavas and Draupadi have already lost kingdom, wealth, and home, yet exile together is bearable: they have each other. When Arjuna leaves for the Himalayas to win weapons, no new material loss occurs — the forest, the hardship, and the sentence are unchanged. What changes is company, and the record's telling is exact: the joy goes out of their lives. A single absence empties what total dispossession together did not. *Interpretation (worker's):* this is the cleanest evidence in the lecture, because it isolates the variable. Remove things and life endures; remove a person and life, as experienced, collapses.

**3. The final test prices a brother above paradise — the close of the epic, Svargarohana movement.**
Yudhishthira alone reaches heaven, is shown his brothers in hell, and refuses return without them: he would rather suffer with those he loves than reign alone. Cecil presents this as the final test — to recognize that heaven alone is no heaven, that paradise is paradise only in company. The record adds the doubling the lecture compresses: at heaven's gate he first refuses to abandon even the stray dog who followed him. The refusal scales from dog to brothers without changing kind. If people were merely means to a good life, heaven would complete the exchange. The poem rejects the exchange: a brother's company is worth more than paradise itself.

**4. The modern inversion is named and inverted back — the money-world movement, same chapter.**
Cecil argues that we think wealth is money, spend money on things, and assume people will follow — the Mercedes with friends, as the record paraphrases it, shoved in the trunk. Because we also hold that people must not be bought, we conclude, in Cecil's short phrase, "de facto people cannot be wealth" (People as true wealth chapter, 24:48). The epic reverses both premises: people are not the byproduct of acquiring things; things are the byproduct at risk of destroying people. Fill the trunk first and you have priced the wealth out of the car.

**5. The causal chain runs in both directions — the wealth-warning movement.**
Cecil gives the mechanism explicitly, and the record draws it as a chain: wealth threatens judgment → judgment fails and people are insulted, treated poorly, or forgotten → they leave → "then all your power goes away." The order matters for the FOR case. Power is not lost first and people second; people are downstream of nothing and power is downstream of them. Keep and honor people, and the earlier links never fire: judgment is corrected by those who "give you wisdom" and "grant you insight," company holds, and power — as a condition of that company — flourishes. Misprice them, and the same chain runs in reverse to emptiness.

**6. Indra and Brihaspati demonstrate the chain in heaven — Indra cycle, Udyoga Parva telling.**
Indra, king of the gods at his height, commits no murder and loses no battle. His insult is omission: he does not rise from his seat when his guru Brihaspati enters, thinking kingship exempts him from respect. Brihaspati reads it, says nothing in anger, and simply leaves. Only then does Indra's power begin to fade and others see he does not know what to do. The record's margin states the sequence plainly: lose the person, lose the power — in that order, always in that order. No enemy defeats Indra; the withdrawal of one honored person unmakes him.

**7. Indra and Vishvarupa show catastrophic mispricing by suspicion — same Indra cycle (Vritra at 33:34 in the question census).**
Priestless, the gods take Vishvarupa — half-asura by his mother — as replacement guru, and under him things return to good. Indra never trusts him and kills him on suspicion of siding with the demons. The record carries the honest hook: in the Udyoga telling Vishvarupa did secretly share offerings with his mother's kin. Carried fairly, the hook strengthens rather than saves Indra's act. In the poem's own ledger the killing remains brahmahatyā, the slaying of a priest, the gravest sin — and its price arrives by name: Tvashta engenders Vritra to avenge his son, and Indra's real fall begins. *Interpretation (worker's):* suspicion treated a functioning, advising person as a threat to be eliminated; the elimination created the actual threat. A person mispriced by fear costs more than the feared disloyalty ever did.

*Where the FOR case is strongest (worker's line):* in the subtraction and the sequence — remove Arjuna and joy leaves an unchanged exile, remove Brihaspati and power drains after wisdom leaves — because there the poem does not assert that people are wealth, it demonstrates what disappears, in order, when they do.

## AGAINST — the counter, at full strength

The draft answer to section 3 says people are "everything" — "the entire content" of a life. At full strength, the record does not sustain that claim. It sustains a narrower, colder one: *certain* people, rightly placed, are the condition of a holder's power, joy, and judgment. That is pricelessness of a kind — but it is not worth as such.

**1. The poem's proof is instrumental.**
In the Indra movement (record: "Indra and the guru who simply left"), Indra fails to rise for Brihaspati; the guru leaves; *then* "Indra's power begins to fade." In the forest movement (record: "wealth is people: the forest without Arjuna"), the exiled brothers discover their misery only when Arjuna departs for the Himalayas. In the chain movement (record: "wealth misleads"), mispriced people leave "and then all your power goes away." *Interpretation:* every demonstration measures what the departed person was doing *for* the one left behind — wisdom, company, power. A proof that runs entirely through consequences to the holder has priced a function, not a person.

**2. The asset frame survives its own critique.**
Cecil's chapter is "People as true wealth" (24:48), and his modern target is the man who gets "the Mercedes Benz" and expects friends to follow, shoved in the trunk. *Interpretation:* replacing money with people as "true wealth" reverses the ranking but keeps the ledger. "People are the truest asset" still asks what a person is *worth to* a life, a court, a king. The trunk has been emptied and its contents promoted — the accounting that put them there is untouched.

**3. The method is recognition-by-loss.**
The guru's worth is demonstrated by withdrawal — he makes himself invisible and cannot be found (record: "the invisible guru"). Arjuna's is demonstrated by absence. Even Yudhishthira's great refusal (record: "the final test") occurs after his brothers have already died on the road. *Interpretation:* a doctrine that can only see worth when it is gone has no instrument for the person standing in front of it, unmissed and unremarkable. It arrives, reliably, too late — which is not a refutation of love, but it is a refutation of this as a *measure* of worth.

**4. The poem prices people by birth throughout.**
In the Mandavya sequel as the record's fork note restores it, Dharma's curse is measured by a servant's womb: Vidura — the counselor everyone consults, who warns against the dice game, and who is ignored "over and over." Karna is the "kind of six": Kunti's hidden eldest, set aside at birth, fighting on the other side, asking his mother at 41:15 why she "left me out in the cold" — and then killed by his own brothers, who did not know he was their brother (record: Krishna's-answer movement). *Interpretation:* in both cases the poem *knows* the man's quality and still lets birth fix his station and his fate. If people were worth everything, unwitnessed excellence would not need a pedigree to count.

**5. The epic spends people by the armyful, and moralizes the spent.**
Krishna's answer to Arjuna is a duty to kill kin in a just war over the kingdom; the record's own count movement totals the result — the hundred sons destroyed, "almost the entire Pandava army" destroyed. Vishvarupa, the replacement guru who advises well and restores things, is suspected for his demon half and killed by a frightened Indra (record: "the invisible guru; Vishvarupa; the killing") — a person processed by a king's fear. On the final road, even Draupadi's fall is assigned a cause: "partiality toward Arjuna" (record: final-test margin). *Interpretation:* anonymous soldiers enter the record as totals; its most wronged woman exits it under a moral demerit. That is not a world in which each person is the entire content of a life. It is a world in which persons are expendable for dharma, dynasty, and lesson.

**6. The chain cuts both ways — the modern objection.**
If worth is proven by the drain when someone withdraws, then a person with nothing to withdraw — no counsel a king needs, no weapons to win, no company whose absence empties a room; the servant-born, the powerless, the merely present — registers, by the poem's own arithmetic, at nothing. A contemporary ethic of unconditional worth exists precisely to cover that person. The epic's chain cannot, because its meter only runs when the powerful feel the loss.

*Where the AGAINST case is strongest (worker's line):* the poem's own evidence proves people's value only instrumentally and only in retrospect — by power draining and joy departing — so the person who can neither leave nor be missed is left worthless by the very arithmetic meant to exalt him.

## CROSS — the two cases compared (coordinator's section)

**Best of FOR, in bullets:**
- The sequence never reverses: in every demonstration on the record the person goes first and the consequence follows — Brihaspati leaves, *then* power fades (Indra movement); Arjuna departs, *then* joy leaves an unchanged exile (forest movement). People are upstream of everything else the poem values; nothing in the record runs the causality the other way.
- The chain is a mechanism, not a mood: wealth → bent judgment → people insulted or forgotten → they leave → power goes (wealth-warning movement). It explains a king of gods falling without a battle, and it predicts in both directions.
- The final test prices company against the highest alternative that exists — paradise — and company wins (Svargarohana movement). Whatever else is instrumental in the poem, that refusal purchases nothing.
- The hardest fact is carried, not hidden: Vishvarupa's secret offerings to his mother's kin stand in the record, and the killing is *still* brahmahatyā in the poem's own ledger, priced by name in Vritra. The poem judges its own king; the FOR case inherits that judgment honestly.

**Best of AGAINST, in bullets:**
- The instrumentality point, unrebutted at the level of proof: every demonstration measures consequences *to the holder* — wisdom withdrawn, joy departed, power drained. A proof that runs entirely through the holder has priced a function, not a person.
- Recognition-by-loss: worth is demonstrated by withdrawal (the invisible guru), absence (Arjuna), and death (the brothers already fallen before the refusal). As a *measure* of worth, the poem's instrument arrives only after the person is gone.
- Birth-pricing is systematic, not incidental: Vidura's excellence is consulted and ignored inside a servant-born station set as a curse's measure; Karna's quality is known and his fate is fixed by birth and secrecy anyway (41:15).
- The asset-frame recoil: "people as true wealth" (24:48) reverses the Mercedes ranking but keeps the ledger — and by the chain's own arithmetic, the person with nothing to withdraw registers at nothing.

**Where each side is strongest:**
- FOR is strongest as a *descriptive* doctrine — how lives, courts, and powers actually rise and fall. No evidence in the record shows power or joy surviving the loss of honoured company; the chain's direction is unbroken across forest, court, and heaven.
- AGAINST is strongest as a doctrine of *worth as such*: the poem proves value-to-the-holder, retrospectively, for persons positioned to be missed. It does not ground the worth of the person who can neither leave nor be missed — the exact person an ethic of worth most needs to cover.

**What remains genuinely open:**
- Whether the final test escapes the instrumental frame. Yudhishthira gains nothing by refusing heaven — no power returns, no company is restored (the brothers are dead; the hell is a vision). If that one act is non-instrumental valuation, it is the record's lone counterweight to the AGAINST's first point — and whether a single act can carry a general doctrine of worth is precisely what is unsettled.
- Whether "wealth" is the poem's category or Cecil's. The record flags "wealth is people" as the lecturer's theme-articulation (chapter 24:48); the AGAINST's ledger critique bites hardest on the *word* wealth. If the poem's own category is something the money-word only approximates, part of the counter is aimed at the translation, not the text — the record does not decide this.
- Consequences as *evidence* of worth versus *ground* of worth: both cases tacitly assume the chain must be one or the other. A reading on which the drain-when-they-leave is how worth becomes visible — not what makes it worth — is constructible from the record's materials (the dog at the gate is worth the refusal before anyone knows he is Dharma), but neither worker was briefed to build it, and the CROSS marks it open rather than decided.
- The servant-born person's worth is asserted by the poem in exactly one way the record shows: Vidura is *consulted by all* — the court knows his value and ignores it anyway. Whether being-known-and-ignored is a valuation the poem endorses or an indictment it files is left, in the record, to the reader.
